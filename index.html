Task 2

Instructional Coaching & Technology Integration Challenge

Grade 3 inquiry · Research → Evaluate → Communicate · discussion starter for the panel

Understanding the need

I would not begin with tools. I would sit with the Grade 3 team first (and join a planning block if useful) to understand:

  • The learning goal — What should students understand and be able to do as researchers and communicators?
  • Current practice — Notebooks, library, talk partners, writing workshop — what already works?
  • The worry under “screen time” — Distraction, shallow copying, equity, or losing talk and play?
  • Confidence map — Who wants to lead, who wants a partner in the room, who wants to watch first?

Then we name success criteria together — for example, students can explain how they know a source is useful. Excellence through clarity; Extraordinary Care for cognitive load.

Purposeful integration (tech optional)

Keep the inquiry arc visible: Ask → Gather → Evaluate → Synthesize → Communicate. Tech only where it improves access, thinking, or authentic audience.

Inquiry moment Low-tech first Tech only if it earns its place
Research Library + curated text sets Shared curated collection — not free-for-all search
Evaluate Useful / not sure / not useful + partner talk Checklist; optional voice note for why
Communicate Oral rehearsal, posters, writing Multimedia only for audience or accessibility

Does this help students research, evaluate, or communicate — or only add screens? If we cannot answer, leave the tool out.

Extra device use for multilingual learners or learning support is equity — not entertainment screen time.

Building teacher capacity

Explore

Observe a co-planned mini-lesson; receive a one-page toolkit.

Try with partner

Co-teach one research or evaluate lesson; I model, then fade.

Embed

Lead independently; brief 10-minute reflection afterward.

Keep support tiny and practical. Grow peer champions. Mixed confidence is normal; shame is not.

Evaluating impact

Look at student learning, not tool counts:

  • Can students explain source usefulness in their own words?
  • Process evidence: notes, sorts, short reflections — not only final products
  • Teacher voice: Did planning feel clearer? Did screen time feel intentional?
  • If tech adds noise, remove it and keep the pedagogy

Success is stronger inquiry thinking and teacher ownership — with technology helping visibly or rightly left aside.
