Task 2

Instructional Coaching & Technology Integration Challenge

Grade 3 inquiry · Research → Evaluate → Communicate · discussion starter for the panel

Understanding the need

I would not begin with tools. I would sit with the Grade 3 team first (and join a planning block if useful) to understand:

  • The learning goal — What should students understand and be able to do as researchers and communicators?
  • Current practice — Notebooks, library, talk partners, writing workshop — what already works?
  • The worry under “screen time” — Distraction, shallow copying, equity, or losing talk and play?
  • Confidence map — Who wants to lead, who wants a partner in the room, who wants to watch first?

Then we name success criteria together — for example, students can explain how they know a source is useful, and communicate understanding in their own words. That is Excellence through clarity, and Extraordinary Care for teacher and student cognitive load.

Supporting purposeful integration (tech is optional)

Keep the inquiry arc visible: Ask → Gather → Evaluate → Synthesize → Communicate. Technology enters only where it improves access, thinking, or authentic audience — never as a default.

Inquiry moment Low-tech / high-value first Tech only if it earns its place
Research Library + curated print/digital text sets Shared curated collection so search isn’t a free-for-all
Evaluate “Useful / not sure / not useful” sorting with partner talk Simple checklist; optional voice note explaining why
Communicate Oral rehearsal, posters, written paragraphs Multimedia only if audience or accessibility needs it

Decision filter: Does this help students research, evaluate, or communicate better — or does it only add screens? If we cannot answer, we leave the tool out.

Extra device use for multilingual learners or learning support (text-to-speech, translation, speech-to-text) is equity — not “more entertainment screens.”

Building teacher capacity

Explore

Observe a co-planned mini-lesson; receive a one-page toolkit.

Try with partner

Co-teach one research or evaluate lesson; I model, then fade.

Embed

Lead independently; brief 10-minute reflection afterward.

Keep support tiny and practical — not a workshop dump. Grow peer champions so confidence spreads sideways. Mixed confidence is normal; shame is not.

Evaluating impact

Look at student learning, not tool counts:

  • Can students explain source usefulness in their own words?
  • Process evidence: notes, sort charts, short reflections — not only final products
  • Teacher voice: Did planning feel clearer? Did screen time feel intentional?
  • If tech adds noise, remove it and keep the pedagogy

Success is stronger inquiry thinking and teacher ownership — with technology either helping visibly or rightly left aside.
