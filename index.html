Task 2

Instructional Coaching & Technology Integration Challenge

Grade 3 inquiry · Research → Evaluate → Communicate · One-page discussion starter for the panel

Understanding the need

I would not begin with tools. I would ask for a short listening conversation with the Grade 3 team (and, if useful, join one existing planning block) to understand:

  • The learning goal — What should students understand and be able to do as researchers and communicators by the end?
  • Current practice — How do they already research, evaluate sources, and share learning (notebooks, library, talk partners, writing workshop)?
  • The worry underneath the screen-time concern — Distraction, shallow copying, equity of access, or loss of talk/play time?
  • Confidence map — Who wants to lead a try, who wants a partner in the room, who wants to watch first?

Only then would we name success criteria together (e.g., students can explain how they know a source is useful; communicate understanding in their own words). That is Excellence through clarity and Extraordinary Care for teacher and student cognitive load.

Supporting purposeful integration (tech is optional)

I would keep the inquiry arc visible: Ask → Gather → Evaluate → Synthesize → Communicate. Technology enters only where it clearly improves access, thinking, or authentic audience — never as a default.

Inquiry moment Low-tech / high-value first Tech only if it earns its place
Research Library + curated print/digital text sets Shared curated collection so search isn’t a free-for-all
Evaluate “Useful / not sure / not useful” sorting with partner talk Simple checklist; optional voice note explaining why
Communicate Oral rehearsal, posters, written paragraphs Multimedia only if audience or accessibility needs it

Decision filter: Does this help students research, evaluate, or communicate better — or does it only increase screen time? If we cannot answer, we leave the tool out.

Extra device use for multilingual learners or learning support (text-to-speech, translation, speech-to-text) is equity — not “more entertainment screens.”

Building teacher capacity (continuum)

Explore

Observe a co-planned mini-lesson; receive a one-page toolkit.

Try with partner

Co-teach one research or evaluate lesson; I model, then fade.

Embed

Lead independently; brief 10-minute reflection afterward.

Keep support tiny and practical — not a workshop dump — and grow peer champions so confidence spreads sideways.

Evaluating impact

We would look at student learning, not tool counts:

  • Can students explain source usefulness in their own words?
  • Process evidence: notes, sort charts, short reflections — not only final products
  • Teacher voice: Did planning feel clearer? Did screen time feel intentional?
  • If tech adds noise, we remove it and keep the pedagogy

Success looks like stronger inquiry thinking and teacher ownership — with technology either helping visibly or rightly left aside.
